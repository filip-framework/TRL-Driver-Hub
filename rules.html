Rules & regulations

Everything you need to know before lights out: race format, points, tiers, driving standards and how stewarding works. Rules are updated between seasons; changes are announced on Discord.

1. Race format

Each tier races once a week on its allocated race night. A standard round consists of an 18-minute one-shot qualifying session followed by a 50% distance Grand Prix with one mandatory pit stop using two tyre compounds (dry conditions).

  • Lobby opens 15 minutes before the session. Be in the lobby and ready 5 minutes before qualifying starts.
  • Formation lap on, standing start, safety car and VSC enabled, full damage and rules.
  • Sprint weekends replace the normal qualifying with a short qualifying that sets the sprint grid. The sprint result sets the Grand Prix grid.
  • If the lobby drops or the session fails before 25% distance the race is restarted from the grid. After 25% the result stands at the last completed lap.

2. Points

Points are awarded to classified finishers in the Grand Prix and the sprint. A driver is classified when they complete at least 90% of the winner's race distance. Disqualified drivers score nothing.

  • Both drivers of a team score for the constructors' championship, as do reserves who stand in for that team.
  • Ties are broken on countback: most wins, then most second places, and so on.

3. Tiers & promotion

The league is split into tiers so that every driver races people of a similar level. Placement for new drivers is based on experience, time-trial pace and availability.

  • Promotion. The top three drivers in each tier are offered a seat in the tier above for the next season.
  • Relegation. The bottom three drivers move down one tier, unless a seat opens up through drivers leaving.
  • Admins may adjust placements for attendance, driving standards or to keep grids full. Decisions are final.

4. Attendance & reserves

  • Full-time drivers are expected to attend every round. Give at least 24 hours' notice in your tier's attendance channel if you cannot race.
  • A reserve driver will be assigned to your car and scores constructors' points for your team. Reserve drivers also score drivers' points in their own name.
  • Two no-shows without notice in a season may result in your seat being reassigned.
  • Reserves who race in three or more rounds get priority for a full-time seat the following season.

5. Driving standards

  • Leave a car's width on the exit of a corner when another car is alongside on the inside or the outside.
  • The overtaking driver must have a significant portion of their car alongside before the braking zone to be entitled to space.
  • One defensive move on the straight is allowed. Moving back to the racing line is permitted only if you leave at least a car's width.
  • Rejoin the track safely. Wait for a gap when returning from a spin or a run-off.
  • Respect blue flags: allow the lapping car through within three corners without losing significant time.
  • Track limits are enforced by the game. Repeated abuse may be reviewed further by the stewards.
  • Do not quit the session after an incident. Retiring to the garage is fine; leaving the lobby is treated as a DNF and may be reviewed.

6. Penalty scale

7. Stewarding & appeals

  • Incidents are reviewed only when reported. Use the incident report form or the stewards' channel within 48 hours of the race.
  • Reports need the lap, the drivers involved and ideally a clip. The stewards may also review the broadcast.
  • Decisions are published on the Penalties page. Provisional results become final 72 hours after the race.
  • You may appeal a decision once, within 24 hours, with new evidence. Appeals are reviewed by a steward who was not involved in the original decision.
  • Stewards never rule on incidents involving themselves.

9. Conduct

  • Be respectful on Discord, on the broadcast and in the lobby. Abuse, harassment or discrimination of any kind will get you removed.
  • Keep race-night voice chat to essential calls. Disputes go to the stewards, not the lobby.
  • Broadcast commentary is neutral. Don't ask the commentators to rule on incidents.
  • Admins reserve the right to make decisions not covered by these rules in the interest of fair racing.
