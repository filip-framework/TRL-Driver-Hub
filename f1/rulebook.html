Part I. Administrative & Financial Regulations

Chapter 1: Overview

1.1 Purpose

This rulebook governs every session run under the F1 banner. It exists so that a driver joining tonight races under the same conditions as a driver who has been here since the first round. The league is built on four commitments:

  • Competition — pace, race craft and consistency decide the championship, not the stewards' room.
  • Integrity — the same rules, applied the same way, to everyone on the grid.
  • Professionalism — organised race nights, published results, and stewarding that explains its decisions.
  • Community — a place where drivers, principals, officials and viewers enjoy racing together.

1.2 Scope and changes

These regulations apply to all divisions, the preseason showcase and any special event announced by Race Control. Race Control may amend the rulebook between rounds; changes are announced on Discord and take effect from the next race night unless stated otherwise.

    2.6 Official results and automation

    • Results are captured from the lobby after every session and published on this site. Published classifications include qualifying, race time, gaps, track-limit warnings converted to time, stewards' penalties and points.
    • Constructors receive the combined championship points of their full-time drivers in each championship event.

    • Results are provisional for 48 hours after publication to allow incident reports and corrections, then final.

    Chapter 3: Driver Regulations

    3.1 Eligibility

    Every driver must be a member of the league Discord, agree to this rulebook before their first session, and race under the same display name on the site, in the lobby and on Discord.

    3.2 Registration

    • New drivers register on the site (F1 driver signup). Before the season they are placed through the driver showcase; mid-season they join the free-agent list until a seat opens.
    • Returning drivers confirm their intent to race before the registration deadline published by Race Control, otherwise their seat may be released.
    • Registering places you on the driver list. A seat is only confirmed once a Team Principal signs you or Race Control assigns you to a division.

    3.3 Driver evaluation and division placement

    Race Control places drivers in the division that matches their pace and experience, using the showcase, time-trial evidence and previous league history. If a driver is found to have misrepresented their pace, Race Control may:

    • Promote the driver immediately to the appropriate division.
    • Remove Constructors' Championship points earned by the team while the placement was incorrect.
    • Deduct further Constructors' Championship points or remove team placement privileges for the following season.
    • Apply additional sporting penalties as required.

    Chapter 4: Team Principals

    4.1 Appointment

    Each constructor is represented by one Team Principal, appointed by Race Control before the season starts. A Team Principal may also be a driver on the roster.

    4.2 Responsibilities

    Team Principals run the constructor day to day, which includes:

    • Managing the salary cap and keeping the constructor within it.
    • Signing and releasing drivers, negotiating contracts and signing reserves.
    • Keeping an active roster in every division the team competes in.
    • Making sure the team meets the financial regulations in Chapter 5.
    • Speaking to Race Control on behalf of the constructor.
    • Making sure their drivers know and follow the sporting regulations and racing code.

    4.3 Driver contracts and transfers

    Contracts may be renewed, terminated or transferred during the off-season transfer window ("silly season"). Drivers whose contracts expire become free agents and may talk to any constructor. Race Control publishes the free-agency dates before each off-season.

    4.4 Mid-season signings

    Seats that open mid-season are filled from the free-agent list. Every mid-season signing is subject to the cap, the waiver rules and Race Control approval.

    6.3 Driver attendance

    • Full-time drivers are expected at every round. Give at least 24 hours' notice in your division's attendance channel if you cannot race so a reserve can be signed.
    • Two unannounced absences in a season release the driver's seat at Race Control's discretion.

    6.4 Equipment responsibility

    Drivers are responsible for their own connection and hardware. A stable connection is a condition of entry; repeated disconnects that affect other drivers may lead to a race ban until the issue is fixed.

    Chapter 7: Race Weekend Procedures

    7.1 Lobby opening

    The lobby opens 20 minutes before the session. Be in the lobby, on the correct setup, five minutes before qualifying starts. Late arrivals start from the back.

    7.2 Qualifying

    One-shot qualifying. Impeding another driver on a flying lap, or setting a time while under investigation, may lead to a grid penalty.

    7.3 Safety car

    Under the safety car, hold position, close up to the car ahead without overtaking and do not weave excessively. Overtaking a car that has lost control is only permitted once it has rejoined.

    7.4 Safety car restart

    The leader controls the restart from the safety-car line. No overtaking before the start/finish line; do not brake-test the pack.

    7.5 Disconnects

    A driver who disconnects is classified where they left the race. If more than a third of the grid disconnects before 25% distance, Race Control may restart the session.

    7.6 Restarts

    Race Control may order a restart for lobby failures or a first-lap incident involving more than half the grid. After 25% distance the result stands as classified.

    7.7 Race classification

    A driver is classified when they complete at least 90% of the winner's distance. Post-race time penalties are added to the finishing time before classification.

    Chapter 8: Racing Code

    8.1 General driving standards

    Race hard, race clean, race predictably. Every driver is responsible for avoiding contact.

    8.2 Overtaking

    The attacking car must have a significant part of its car alongside before the braking zone to be owed space. Divebombs from far back are the attacker's responsibility.

    8.3 Defensive driving

    One defensive move on a straight. A driver may move back towards the racing line only if a full car's width is left for the attacker.

    8.4 Corner rights

    A car alongside at the apex is entitled to a car's width on the exit. Pushing a car off on exit, or squeezing on entry, is a penalisable offence.

    8.5 Rejoining the circuit

    Rejoin safely and parallel to the track, yielding to cars on the racing line. Rejoining into traffic is treated as causing a collision.

    8.6 Blue flags

    Lapped cars must let the leaders through within three corners of a blue flag without losing significant time. Racing the lapping car is an offence.

    8.7 Unsportsmanlike conduct

    Retaliation, deliberate collisions, quitting after contact to deny evidence, abuse in chat or on voice, and manipulating results all carry the heaviest penalties in Appendix A, up to removal from the league.

    Chapter 9: Stewarding & Judicial Process

    9.1 Incident reports

    Incidents are only investigated when reported. Submit reports in the stewards' channel within 48 hours of the race with the lap, drivers involved and a description.

    9.2 Evidence

    Clips, broadcast timestamps and replays are all accepted. Reports without evidence may be dismissed if the stewards cannot find the incident on the broadcast.

    9.3 Steward review

    A panel of at least two stewards reviews each report. A steward never rules on an incident involving themselves or their constructor.

    9.4 Penalties

    Penalties are published on the results page and on the driver's profile. Time penalties apply to the finished race; grid drops apply to the next round the driver starts; licence points accumulate as set out in Chapter 3.4.

    9.5 Appeals

    A decision may be appealed once, within 24 hours, with new evidence. A steward who did not sit on the original panel reviews the appeal. The outcome of an appeal is final.
